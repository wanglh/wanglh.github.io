为了重建成人学习者的信心，大学必须首先赢得他们的信任

原文标题: To rebuild adult learner confidence, universities must first earn their trust

First-time college enrollment among adults declinednearly 16 percentin fall 2025, ending a four-year streak of post-pandemic growth.

The decline comes as colleges and universities are already facing enrollment pressure fromfewer high school studentspursuing four-year degrees, making adult learners an increasingly important population for sustaining enrollment.

It’s not that adults have stopped valuing higher education. Rather, they’re making enrollment decisions in a very different environment than they were even a few years ago. AI is reshaping the workforce, career paths are rapidly evolving, and the long-term value of various credentials feels harder to predict.

This presents an opportunity for universities to rethink how they engage prospective adult learners. In a moment defined by uncertainty, institutions can help students navigate their options with greater clarity, transparency, and support.

Why adult learners are hitting pause

Part of the enrollment decline reflects a natural post-pandemic correction. In the years immediately following COVID-19, more adults returned to school after experiencing layoffs, career disruptions, and shifts in the job market. As that wave of adult learners levels off, institutions are no longer seeing the same enrollment momentum.

At the same time, AI is transforming industries, making it harder for professionals to predict which skills will remain valuable and which educational path will best position them for long-term success. Rather than moving straight into enrollment, many adults are taking a more deliberate approach.

The growing number of certificates, bootcamps, employer-sponsored training programs, and other alternative credentials has only made the decision more complex. With an abundance of options, prospective students face greater pressure to determine which educational investment will deliver the greatest return.

Even the motivations that inspire adults to return to school can become the reasons they hesitate. A professional seeking a promotion may worry about balancing coursework with a full-time job. A parent hoping to improve their family’s financial future may question whether the upfront cost is worth it. Someone looking to transition into a growing field may struggle to identify which credential will open the right doors.

Together, these challenges underscore that the question is no longer whether higher education is worthwhile — it’s whether a particular pathway is the right investment at this moment in a student’s career.

Rather than focusing solely on persuading more adults to enroll, universities should focus on supporting informed decisions. By helping prospective students connect their educational goals to meaningful career outcomes, institutions can become trusted partners in one of the biggest decisions adult learners will make.

3 ways to rebuild confidence among adult learners

Universities can build prospective students’ confidence by reducing uncertainty at every stage of the enrollment journey. Here are three ways to do it.

  1. Create a clear path from investment to opportunity

Now more than ever, adult learners want to understand how a program will help them achieve their goals and whether it’s worth the investment.

Universities should be transparent upfront about what matters most: the credential students will earn, the cost and duration of the program, the skills they’ll gain, and the career opportunities those skills can unlock.

For example, clearly outline how a healthcare management program prepares graduates for leadership roles by cultivating strategic decision-making and team management skills. Or highlight how a nine-month data analytics certificate can also award credit toward a future master’s degree, giving students the flexibility to continue their education when the time is right.

By helping prospective students understand how a program can lead to tangible career outcomes, institutions make it easier for them to move forward. In turn, institutions become trusted partners in helping students achieve their career aspirations, not just a place to earn a credential.

  1. Lower the stakes of taking the first step

For many adults, returning to school can feel like an all-or-nothing decision. Universities can make taking the first step more appealing by creating pathways that reduce the perceived risk of enrolling.

Stackable certificates that count toward a future degree are one example. Rather than asking students to commit to a multi-year program, institutions can allow them to earn a credential with immediate workforce value while keeping the door open for continued education.

Flexible scheduling, online learning options, and multiple entry points can provide similar reassurance by making it easier to balance education alongside work and family responsibilities.

When adult learners can build momentum without feeling like they’re putting their lives on hold, taking the first step feels far less daunting. That flexibility empowers prospective students to begin their educational journey on terms that fit their lives.

  1. Make the enrollment journey more personal

Sometimes, questions about cost or scheduling can mask deeper concerns about belonging or the ability to succeed after years away from the classroom.

That’s why outreach should go beyond promoting programs. Dedicated enrollment specialists, personalized guidance, and opportunities to ask questions of existing students, alumni, and faculty can help adult learners navigate uncertainty before they commit.

Whether it’s explaining transfer credits, mapping out a realistic course schedule, or discussing work and family responsibilities, these conversations show prospective students that the institution is invested in their success from the very beginning. That reassurance can make the difference between hesitation and feeling confident enough to move forward.

Meet adult learners where they are

Higher education hasn’t become less relevant for adult learners. If anything, it’s become more important as careers, industries, and workforce expectations continue to evolve.

But adult learners aren’t looking for institutions that simply promise opportunity. Instead, they’re looking for ones that help them choose a strategic path forward.

Universities that take on this role can build trust with students long before they enter the classroom. And that trust is what helps institutions attract, enroll, and retain the adult learners shaping the future of higher education.
